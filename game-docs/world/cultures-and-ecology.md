# Cultures and ecology: how islands, mons and people shape each other

Status: Proposed. Jaedan's direction (Decided): every island has its own culture, and the way it treats its stone and its mons runs through everything. This doc is the framework every island sheet uses.

## 1. The chain

Every island is a loop, not a list. Change one link and the others move.

1. **Land and source shape the mons.** Mons absorb the element that pools where they live (see `11-story.md`, The Current). A volcanic island grows fire mons, a reef grows water mons.
2. **Mons shape the people.** What lives next to you decides what you eat, how you work, what you fear and what you worship.
3. **People shape the mons back.** They breed some, hunt some, protect some and overharvest some. Over generations that changes the mons themselves.
4. **Mons shape the land.** They are the release valve for the Current. They graze, burrow, pollinate and spread energy around. Remove them and the element builds up.
5. **The stone sits at the source** and shapes the island's politics: who guards it, who profits from it, who fears it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Harmony is a balance, not a peace.** Every island has found a balance that works, and that balance usually has a cost someone is paying. The wound, the ban and the heir's crew are what knock balances over.

## 2. How people shape mons (Proposed)

This is where the "every creature is an individual" pillar gets its reasons. Regional differences are not random; they come from how people have treated that species.

| How people treat a species | What it does to the mons over time | What you notice in play |
|---|---|---|
| **Bred and worked** | Bigger, calmer, coat and colour variants | Approaches you. Variants you won't see elsewhere |
| **Revered** | Bold, unafraid of people, often old and large | Ignores you or walks right up. Catching may be forbidden |
| **Hunted or harvested** | Smaller, warier, more nocturnal | Flees early. Rare by day |
| **Feared and walled out** | Aggressive, territorial at the edges of town | Attacks first. Wardens and walls |
| **Ignored** | Wild baseline | Normal temperament |

The same species can sit in two rows on two islands. That is the point: the bull calf on a farming island and the bull calf on the volcano should feel different.

## 3. How each island relates to its stone (Proposed)

After the ban, every keeper tradition drifted in its own direction. Each island has one main relationship and sometimes a second.

| Relationship | What it looks like | Tension it carries |
|---|---|---|
| **Faith** | The stone is holy. A priesthood, rites, pilgrims. The keeper is a priest | Doctrine has replaced the real reason. They may refuse to use the stone even to save themselves |
| **Economy** | The stone and its store are wealth. Guarded by a guild, taxed, used as collateral | Who profits. The stone has a price, so someone will sell it |
| **Law** | Guarding it is civic duty. Registers, inspections, strict penalties for awakening | Bureaucracy that forgot why. Punishes the people trying to help |
| **Folk** | Half-forgotten. Superstitions, a festival nobody can explain | Easy to steal from. Nobody takes the threat seriously |
| **Secret** | Hidden even from most locals. A family or order keeps it | Who gets to know. Easy for the true antagonist to infiltrate |
| **Contested** | Two factions both claim it | Open conflict. The heir's crew can exploit the split |

## 4. How each island relates to its mons (Proposed)

| Relationship | Example |
|---|---|
| **Partners** | Mons work beside people: hauling, fishing, pollinating, guarding |
| **Revered** | Mons as ancestors or spirits. Shrines, taboos |
| **Resource** | Farmed or harvested: shed scales, silk, fruit, milk, heat |
| **Feared** | Walls, wardens, curfews. Life organised around danger |
| **Rivals** | People and mons compete for the same land or food |

## 5. Politics between islands (Proposed)

- **The ban is enforced unevenly.** Law islands enforce it hard, folk islands barely know it exists. Some quietly break it.
- **Trade follows elements.** Fire islands trade heat and smithing, reef islands trade food, forest islands trade medicine. Islands depend on each other more than they admit.
- **Suspicion after the theft.** When the record of every island's store goes missing, islands start blaming each other. Old rivalries come back.
- **Not everyone fears the surge.** Some islands would profit from a neighbour's ruin. That is fertile ground for the true antagonist.
- **The ruined island is a shared wound.** Every culture tells its own version of the disaster, and they don't agree.

## 6. How this becomes gameplay (Proposed)

| Culture choice | Gameplay effect |
|---|---|
| How people treat a species | Its temperament, size and variants on that island (wild behaviour system) |
| Faith or law islands | Catching rules: some species can't be caught here, or need permission |
| Economy islands | Shops, prices and what you can buy or sell |
| Stone relationship | The keeper's trial: a rite, a contract, an exam, a festival, a test of trust |
| Feared mons | Danger zones, curfews, night events |
| Inter-island politics | Side stories, reputation, who helps you later |

## 7. Each island's local problem comes from a broken link

Use the chain to write every island's own story. Pick the link that broke:

- **Land broke:** the wound leaks into the source. Mons sicken, the element runs wild.
- **Mons broke:** a species is overharvested, revered into overpopulation, or driven out.
- **People broke:** a faction, a guild, a feud, a law that no longer makes sense.
- **Stone broke:** stolen, sold, contested, or nobody remembers where it is.

Fixing the link is how you earn the keeper's trust.

## 8. Three sketches to show the chain (examples, not decisions)

**A volcanic island (fire bulls).** The bulls graze the lava fields and soak up heat, which keeps eruptions small. People revere them as hearth-bringers and the stone is holy. Their faith forbids catching bulls, which is why bulls there are bold and huge. *Broken link:* the heir's crew has been culling bulls for forced awakenings, so the mountain is getting angrier.

**A reef island (water mons).** Divers harvest scales that water mons shed. The stone belongs to a merchant guild that rents out diving rights. *Broken link:* the guild pushed harvesting too far, the reef mons are stressed, and the bleaching everyone blames on the wound is half their own doing.

**A forest island (fruit bats).** The bats pollinate the orchards, so the whole island lives on night hours, with markets that open at dusk. The stone is half-forgotten, a lantern festival nobody can explain. *Broken link:* the bats are leaving because the wound is spoiling the fruit, and nobody connects the festival to the stone.
