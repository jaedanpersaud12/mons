# Story, tone and structure

Updated 15 November 2026 (Rules layer reviewed). Markers: **Decided** (Jaedan chose it), **Default** (proposed and not objected to), **Open**.

Every name in *italics* is a placeholder.

## 1. The game in one paragraph

A boy from a coastal town is drawn into a race across an island chain after a stranger steals his town's stone and the record of where every island keeps its own. The stranger is the heir of the man who destroyed an island a century ago, and he is trying to stop the same disaster coming again, using the same method that caused it last time. The hero must earn the trust of twelve island keepers, bring the old bond between people and creatures back, and help a whole region hold back a once-a-century surge of elemental energy with bond instead of force.

## 2. Theme (Decided)

**Doing the right thing the wrong way.**

The ancestor forced the creatures to save the islands and destroyed one instead. The elders banned awakening to stop that happening again, and in doing so removed the islands' defences. The heir is forcing the creatures again to save the islands. Each generation is right about the danger and wrong about the method. The hero's job is to break the cycle, not just to win.

## 3. Tone (Decided)

**Light-hearted on the surface, serious underneath.** The reference is *Avatar: The Last Airbender*: jokes, warmth and goofy side characters on top of war, loss and a kid carrying something huge. Also *Pokémon Mystery Dungeon: Explorers of Sky* and *Wind Waker*.

Many things are taken from *Clair Obscur: Expedition 33*, but not its heavier tone.

**Red Dead Redemption 2** is a reference for how alive the world feels: people with their own routines and side stories, a camp that feels like home, and a world that keeps going whether you are watching or not. (Decided)

### Tone rules (Decided)

1. **Don't force the humour.** Mum, the rival, townsfolk and the creatures' personalities carry it, but lightly. Try jokes, see how they feel, cut what doesn't land. Bleached reefs and empty villages are shown straight.
2. **Show the serious side, don't announce it.** Storytelling 101, and the most important rule here. A keeper who guards a stone and can't remember why. A house on the ruined island with the table still set.
3. **The darker side is there from the start, but never pushed.** Like the real world: hard things are part of how things are, and you notice them from the first island. The stolen starter's forced form is a moment to take in, not the point where the game stops smiling for good.
4. **Never undercut a heavy beat with a joke straight after.** Let it breathe. A camp scene later brings the warmth back.
5. **Most people have reasons. Some are just jerks.** The heir is grieving and certain, and most of his crew believe him. But not every bad actor needs a sympathetic backstory.

### Avoid

The doom-on-a-countdown idea is also central to *Clair Obscur*. Keep the Long Storm rooted in this world's own rules (the Current, the bond) so it doesn't read as a copy.

## 4. How the world works

Pokémon never explains where its creatures' power comes from. This game does, and everything below follows from one rule.

### The Current (Decided, name Open)

Elemental energy moves through the world like water does.

1. It **wells up at sources**: volcanic vents, deep reefs, ancient root systems.
2. It **flows through the land**.
3. **Creatures absorb it** by eating, drinking and basking, and their bodies adapt to whatever element pools where they live.
4. It is **released back into the land** through battle, rest and death, and seeps back down to the sources.

### What the rule explains

| Thing | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Types | A creature's **main type** is the element it absorbs, so main types match biomes: the fire bulls live on the volcano because they are built from it. Its optional **secondary type** comes from where and how it lives inside that biome: a deep-water mon is Water / Dark, a flyer from the storm peaks is Lightning / Flying. (Decided) |
| Evolution | A creature evolves when it has absorbed more than its body can hold. It grows new structures to carry the extra. This is why each evolution adds features rather than only size. The lava bull's cracks are vents |
| Battles | Two creatures throwing stored energy at each other |
| The type chart | How elements react: water quenches fire, roots break stone |
| Creatures' place in nature | They are the world's release valve. They spread energy around so it never builds up in one place |
| Stones | Places where the Current pooled so densely it set solid, like salt left when seawater dries. **They keep forming slowly at every source** |

### Awakening (Decided, name Open)

Our version of Mega Evolution. "Mega Evolution" is Pokémon's term and is not used.

- **Awakening belongs to species, not individuals.** Twelve species in this region evolved the structures to take a stone's full charge. There are many of each in the wild. They are caught and raised like any other creature. **None of them are legendaries.**
- A stone pours more energy into a creature than its body can hold.
- **With a deep bond, the creature lets its partner carry part of the strain.** Like two people carrying a beam that would crush one of them, the bond physically shares the load.
- **Forced** (without a bond), the creature carries all of it. It burns out, and the excess spills into the land.
- **Anyone** with that species' stone and a deep enough bond can awaken it: the player, keepers, rivals, and eventually ordinary trainers.

### Awakening rules in play (Decided)

- Needs that species' stone as a held item **and** a high bond with that individual creature. A freshly caught one cannot do it.
- One awakening per battle. It lasts until the creature faints or the battle ends.
- Bond is earned in battle and in the world: how you approach creatures, time in your party, not letting them faint, and quiet camp scenes (see section 11).
- The twelve species live in your normal party and storage. **No separate bag.** Use any of them on your team.
- Forced forms (used by the heir's side) are stronger but unstable: disobedience, recoil, and a look that is visibly wrong.
- Forms follow the creature design rules: they add features and command respect.

### The Long Storm (Decided, name Open)

The Current is tidal. About once a century it **surges**, the way a king tide comes in higher than normal.

The original purpose of awakening was not fighting. During a surge, **bonded, awakened creatures of the twelve species stood at the twelve sources** and soaked up the excess together. Many drains, not one pump.

## 5. History

### About 100 years ago (Decided)

1. The old bonds between people and creatures had faded, and few people could still awaken.
2. The surge came. **The ancestor** forced a handful of powerful creatures to hold it back. He was trying to save the islands (Decided: desperation, not ambition).
3. The creatures burned out. The excess tore through, and **an island was lost**. (Which element ran wild is Open.)
4. That tear, **the wound**, never closed. It still leaks raw Current.

### After the disaster (Decided)

- The elders concluded that the bond cannot be checked from outside, so anyone holding a stone could try.
- **They banned awakening.** Since stones keep forming, someone had to gather them before anyone else found them.
- Each island got a **keeper** whose job is to collect the stones that island grows and lock them away. Keepers still bond with one partner of their island's species, privately, as tradition.
- After a century, it is a tradition that has outlived its reason. Most keepers do not know why they do it, like a village that never builds on one hill "because grandmother said so", when the hill is a floodplain.
- Without meaning to, the ban removed the region's defence against the next surge.
- **Only the ancestor's family kept the full record**, including when the surge returns.

### Now (Decided)

- The surge is due again. Almost no one knows.
- The wound has been worsening: ash drifting, reefs bleaching, creatures fleeing their homes. This is why the warden has rescues at all.
- The heir knows exactly what is coming. He is right about the danger, and he is about to repeat his ancestor's method.

**More cast wanted (Decided).** The world has to feel alive, with people on every island who have their own lives, routines and small stories, RDR2 style. The table above is only the core.

**Why the warden and not a professor:** a secret herd of rare creatures kept in a small town for generations makes no sense, since the animals are mortal. Recent rescues waiting to go home explain why three rare creatures from far-off biomes are in town at all.

## 7. The rival (Decided, except where marked)

- **The warden's kid.** The theft happened at their parent's station, possibly because they left it unlocked. They feel responsible.
- Sets out to take a rescue home, and to get the stolen one back.
- Their starter chooses them on its home island, just like yours.
- **The guilt makes them want a shortcut.** (Open: Jaedan is unsure. With the switch-up in section 14, the temptation may come from the true antagonist instead of the heir.)
- **They come back.** Their return is the first proof that bond beats force, and they stand at a source in the finale.

## 8. The starters (Decided)

The three starters are the warden's current rescues: the fire bull calf, the water wader chick and the plant bat pup. Each is far from home.

| Who | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Yours** | You agree to carry it home |
| **The rival's** | The rival takes another for the same reason (Decided: the one strong against yours) |
| **The third** | Stolen by the heir on the night of the theft. Returns later, **forcibly awakened**, as a boss |

### Home chapters (Decided)

- Each starter's **home island is one island on the map**. Everyone can visit all three.
- On your starter's home island it is free to stay, and it **chooses you**. This is the bond payoff.
- On the other two islands that scene does not play. The cost is three cutscene variants, not three routes.
- You witness the rival's home chapter as well.
- Suggested placement (Open): wader's coast in tier 1, bat's forest in tier 2, bull's volcano in tier 3.

## 9. The twelve awakening species (Decided)

All twelve are ordinary species: catchable, raisable and found in the wild in their home areas.

| Count | Which | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | The three starter lines | Rare in the wild and far from town, but they exist there |
| 1 | The home island's species | An early-route common. Surprise: the creature you caught on route 1 can awaken |
| 7 | One per other source island | Each keeper's partner and trial ace |
| 1 | The lost island's species | Thought extinct since the disaster. A small population survives on the ruined island. Rare, not legendary |

### What the player needs

- **Earn all twelve keepers' stones.** The stones are the badges.
- **Raise whichever species you like.** You do not need to own all twelve.
- The finale needs bonded, awakened creatures of every species at every source. Those come from **everyone**: keepers, the rival, trainers you met along the way, and you.

### Who can awaken over the course of the game (Decided)

| When | Who |
|---|---|
| Start | Keepers (bonded, in secret) and the heir's crew (forced) |
| As you earn stones | You. Keepers start teaching again |
| Act 3 onward | Ordinary trainers. Battles get harder, and the world visibly changes |

### Room to grow (Decided)

- **The twelve species** come from the region's twelve great sources.
- **Lesser stones** form at weaker pools: a hot spring, a lightning-struck peak, an old grove. More species can awaken with these, but those forms cannot anchor a surge. Side areas and post-game.
- **Other regions** have their own sources, species and surge cycles, so expansions add to the world without changing it.
- Long-term ceiling: about 500 creatures and 40 forms. **Planned shipping scope: about 100 creatures and 12 forms**, growing only once the engine pipeline can import rigged models cheaply.

## 10. Structure: how you travel (Decided)

Modelled on *Clair Obscur*'s world map: linear levels joined by an overworld, gated by traversal upgrades. **The sea is the overworld, your boat is the traversal, the islands are the levels.**

| Step | What it is |
|---|---|
| Home | Fixed. The theft. You get your boat |
| Tier 1 | 3 islands, any order |
| Midpoint | Fixed. The stolen starter returns forced. Boat upgrade: reef hull |
| Tier 2 | 3 islands, any order |
| Reveal | Fixed. The heir is right. Boat upgrade: storm sail |
| Tier 3 | 2 islands, any order |
| The ruined island | Fixed. The surge and the finale |

Twelve islands, one source each.

### Rules

- **The main plot only advances on fixed islands.** Each open-tier island's own story (its keeper, its local problem, its signs of the wound) works in any order.
- **Keepers scale to how many stones you hold**, not to which island they are on. Your first trial is always the easiest. (Decided.)
- **Sail freely to anywhere you have unlocked**, including home.
- **Scouting:** you can see and catch creatures near later islands before you can land. Raise and bond with them early, so the stone, when you earn it, is a payoff and not a wall.

### Later (Open)

- **Late islands get raided.** Islands you put off are visited by the heir's crew first, and their chapter changes. It makes your order matter but doubles the writing. Hold until the base game works.

## 11. Ideas taken from other games (Open, to discuss in systems)

| Idea | How it fits |
|---|---|
| **Reactive turn-based combat** (*Clair Obscur*) | Turn-based, but you dodge and parry incoming attacks with timed inputs. Fixes the "waiting your turn" problem of Fire Red battles |
| **Camp scenes** (*Clair Obscur*) | Rest, talk to your party, watch your mons interact. A natural place for bond to grow |
| **World map with traversal upgrades** | Taken (section 10). From *Clair Obscur* |
| **A living world** | People with daily routines, ambient conversations, strangers with small side stories, events that happen without you. From *RDR2* |

## 12. Plot

### Act 1: Home

1. **Wake up** (Built). Mum gets him up. He promised to walk to the gate today.
2. **The gate.** A stranger arrives and asks the elders about the old bond and the stone. He is refused.
3. **The warden's station.** The hero meets the three rescues and the warden's kid.
4. **That night,** the stranger steals the town's stone store, the record of every island's store, and one of the rescues.
5. **Morning.** The elders admit a fragment of the truth. The warden asks the hero and the rival to carry the two remaining rescues home. The hero gets a boat.

### Act 2: Tier 1 to the midpoint

Each open island follows a pattern:

- Arrive and see signs of the wound.
- Find out whether the heir's crew got there first.
- Earn the keeper's trust and stone in their trial.
- Learn one more fragment of the history.
- A starter home chapter, where one applies.

**Midpoint:** the stolen starter appears, forcibly awakened, and fights you as a boss. (Open: does it join you, go home, or stay with the heir until the end?)

### Act 3: Tier 2 to the reveal

- The rival is tempted and sides with the heir for a while, then returns.
- The comic duo starts to doubt.
- Keepers begin teaching again, and ordinary trainers start to awaken.
- **The reveal:** the pieces come together. The surge is coming and the heir is right. The confrontation becomes about method, not motive.

### Act 4: Tier 3 to the ruined island

- The surge begins.
- The heir attempts the forced version, repeating the ancestor's mistake on the same ground.
- The hero finds the lost island's surviving species.
- **Finale:** bonded trainers from across the region stand at all twelve sources and hold the surge together. The cycle is broken.
- The heir faces what he almost did. (Open: redemption, and whether he helps at the end.)

### Post-game (Decided)

Lesser stones and their forms. Side areas. The wider world beyond the chain.

## 14. The switch-up (Proposed by Jaedan)

**The idea:** at some point the heir comes over to your side. The real mission becomes holding back the storm, and the true antagonist is someone taking advantage of it, pushing for the surge to hit.

### How it fits what is already decided

- **The theme still holds.** The heir was doing the right thing the wrong way. The switch is the moment he sees it.
- **The world rules already support it.** Forcing an awakening dumps the excess Current into the land. Anyone who wants a bigger surge *wants* the heir forcing creatures. His method is the trap.
- **Tone rule 5 makes room for it.** The true antagonist doesn't need a sympathetic backstory.
- **Natural place for it: the Reveal island**, end of Act 3. "The heir is right" becomes "the heir is being used".

### Who is the true antagonist? (Open)

1. **Someone inside the heir's crew.** His adviser or backer, who taught him the forced method knowing it feeds the storm. Maybe the ancestor had someone like that too, so the same hand is behind both disasters.
2. **A disaster profiteer.** Wants the surge to wreck the islands so they can buy them, rebuild them and own them. Outside money. A plain jerk, and very real-world.
3. **A power seeker.** Raw Current from the wound makes forced forms far stronger. They want the surge so the ban breaks for good and the strongest rule.
4. **Someone at home.** An elder or keeper who has known all along and is letting it come.

These can combine: one is *who*, another is *why*. For example, 1 for who and 2 or 3 for why.

### What it changes

| Thing | Change |
|---|---|
| Act 3 and Act 4 | The reveal flips the heir. Act 4 is you, the heir and the region against the true antagonist and the storm |
| The heir's ending | Mostly answered: he is redeemed and joins. Open: what it costs him |
| The stolen starter | Its fate likely turns on the heir's switch |
| The rival | The shortcut may come from the true antagonist instead of the heir |
| The comic duo | Their switch can mirror the heir's, earlier and smaller |
| The finale | The heir stands at a source too, the bonded way, breaking his family's cycle |
