# The game (Godot 4.6)

The game opens on **Fernhollow harbour**, the home town, on a morning. The test meadow it grew from is still here: run with `-- --scene=world` to load it.

## What is in the town

- **Your buildings:** the cottage, the cabins, the workshop hall, the windmill, the well, the gate, lamps, benches, signs, mailboxes, oaks, pines and bushes are the ones built for the browser version of Fernhollow, lifted out as individual assets (see "Assets" below).
- **The place:** the cottage with the fruit stall, the path to the pier and its rowing boat, the harbour wall and market, red-roofed houses, the tower, the warden's hall with a pen beside it, the windmill on the bluff, and the road out to the gate through tall grass.
- **The water:** the bay uses the same see-through water as the lake (banded depth colours, foam at the waterline, glints, wading, floating, rings and splashes). Past the shallows it runs to the horizon.
- **The sky:** a gradient sky with sun, moon, stars, drifting clouds, and other islands of the chain on the horizon. It is painted by the pixel pass wherever no splat was drawn.
- **Time of day:** the clock runs through a full day in 12 minutes. Light is a colour grade over the baked morning light, and the palette is graded to match, so the picture stays on-palette at any hour. At night windows and lanterns light up and lanterns throw rings of light on the ground. Shadows do not move with the sun.
- **Who is about:** nine townsfolk on their own rounds (the fruit seller, a fisherman on the pier, a sweeper on the quay, two neighbours at the well, the warden, a child, a market trader, an elder). Walk up to one and they stop, turn and say something. At dusk they walk home and go in; at dawn they come back out.
- **Creatures:** the bull calf is in the warden's pen. Two strays live in town: one dozes on the quay, one keeps raiding the fruit stall and getting shooed off. The strays are the calf's body, small and recoloured, standing in until they have models of their own. The townsfolk are the hero's body in other clothes for the same reason.
- **Small things:** chimney smoke, washing on a line, nets drying, the windmill turning, the boat rocking.

## Run it

1. Open Godot 4.6 (4.3 or newer should work).
2. Import this folder (`project.godot`).
3. Press Play (F5).

The project uses the **Compatibility** renderer.

## Run it without Godot

`build\Mons.exe` is a single-file Windows build. It is not committed (it is about 150 MB), so it only exists where it has been built or copied.

To rebuild it after a change, double-click `build_windows.bat`. That needs Godot 4.6 with its export templates installed; the script's first lines say how to point it at Godot. The export settings are in `export_presets.cfg`.

Windows will warn about an unknown publisher the first time, because the file is not signed. Choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

## Assets

`assets/` holds 21 splat assets, one file each (`home.splat`, `cabin_red.splat`, `hall.splat`, `mill.splat`, `oak.splat`, `lamp.splat` and so on), with sizes and bounds in `assets/assets.json`. They were exported from the Fernhollow Splat Town artifact by `tools/export_assets.py`, which opens the artifact in headless Chrome and saves each building as the browser build baked it, relit for this game's sun. That only needs re-running if the artifact changes.

The town is put together in `tools/bake_fernhollow.py`: each asset is placed by position, turn and scale (`W.place("cabin_red", x, z, yaw)`), on ground levelled under it. The big buildings are thinned to about half their splats to keep the frame rate up. Still generated by the script, not assets: the land, the bay, the harbour wall, the market stalls, the pier, the boats and the blue-roofed tower.

Next step for this pipeline: place assets in a Godot scene by hand instead of by coordinates in the script.

## Rebuilding the town

`python3 tools/bake_fernhollow.py` (from `tools/`, needs numpy) rebuilds `data/town_*.bin`, `data/town.json` and `data/town_palette.json`. Everything in the town is generated by that script: terrain, buildings, props, where people stand and what they say. Change it there and re-run.

## What to check

- **Look:** does it match the browser Test Meadow?
- **Speed:** what frame rate do you get?
- **Flicker:** any black dots or shimmer in the swaying grass?

## How it works

| Piece | File | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Shared splat code | `shaders/splat.gdshaderinc` | Wind sway, contact shadows, bones and effects, disc shape, depth |
| Depth prepass | `shaders/*_prepass.gdshader` | Writes each splat's depth, pushed back slightly. Leaves colour untouched |
| Blend pass | `shaders/*_add.gdshader` | Lays each splat over the view with a soft-edged alpha, only where it is close to the front surface |
| Opaque pass | `shaders/*_depth.gdshader` | Opaque mode only: one pass, colour written directly |
| Pixel pass | `shaders/post.gdshader` | Divides blended colour by coverage, fills pinholes, snaps to the 32-colour palette with dither |
| Scene | `main.gd` | Loads the data, builds the meshes, runs the player, the calf and the camera |

**One low-resolution view.** The world is drawn at 400 lines into a transparent floating-point view. rgb ends up as blended colour times coverage and alpha as coverage; the pixel pass divides one by the other. (The first version used two views and drew everything four times; this draws it twice.)

**Splats are points.** Each map cell is two meshes of points: the first half of its splats and the second half. Godot culls cells outside the camera's view. Per-splat data rides in three custom vertex attributes as floats.

**Detail.** Half detail hides the second mesh of every cell and enlarges the rest to cover. Switching is instant. With auto detail on, the game drops to half if it cannot hold 55 fps and returns to full when it has clear headroom.

**The calf and the player** each use 16 bone matrices passed to the shader. Every splat belongs to one bone and blends toward that bone's parent, so joints bend.

## Water

The water is see-through, and it is not drawn at all. The lake has a real bed with rocks, corals and sea plants standing on it, and the shader tints anything below the waterline by how deep it is. That is why you can see the bed, and why the player's legs change colour when wading.

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| Lakebed bands | The bed is drawn in flat bands of the lake's own colours, one per depth (pale aqua, aqua, teal, blue, navy), so it stays clean after the pixel pass. Shadowed and weedy spots take the next colour down |
| Depth tint | On everything else, the water's colour lies over things like a veil: thin at the surface, thicker with depth. Things keep their own colour underneath, so a pink coral stays pink and a rock stays dark |
| Lakebed | Sand that follows the terrain, with weedy patches, and a deep hole in the middle |
| On the bed | Rocks (a few break the surface), branching corals, brain corals, tube sponges, starfish, sea grass that sways, tall kelp on the walls of the hole. Each darkens the sand beside it, so it reads as sitting on the bottom |
| Light lines | Thin moving lines of light that sit in the same place whatever is under them, which is what makes the surface read as lying on top. Shallows only |
| Shimmer | Things under water wobble slightly, more the deeper they are |
| Foam and glints | A thin layer of surface splats that the shader only shows as foam at the waterline or as pinprick glints |
| Waterline | Moves in and out, leaving wet sand behind it |

Key 3 switches to the old flat, opaque blue for comparison.

### The player in the water

| What | How it behaves |
|---|---|
| Wading | The deeper you stand, the slower you walk and the slower you start and stop. At chest depth you move at about half speed |
| Floating | Past shoulder depth your feet leave the bottom and you float, bobbing slightly, a little slower again. You can cross the deep hole |
| Jumping | A jump out of water is weaker the deeper you are |
| Rings | Rings spread from you as you move, closer together the faster you go. Standing still gives a slow ring now and then |
| Splashes | Droplets fly up when you land in water or run in from the shore |
| Foam collar | A broken ring of foam sits where your body meets the surface |
| Under the surface | The part of you below the waterline takes the water's colour |

Nothing in the water is solid yet: you pass through rocks and corals. There is no swimming animation; floating reuses the walk. The calf still stays out of the water.

The water code is `water_tint()` and the `g == 5.0` block in `shaders/splat.gdshaderinc`. `tools/bake_water.py` is the script that rebuilt the lake (bed, props, surface layer, palette) from the original export; it has already been run on the data in this folder.

**Palette.** The lake has eight colours of its own (deep blue to pale aqua, a slate for rocks, one lilac and one pink for anything living), and the tint is tuned so that what is under water lands on them. That is why the palette is 32 colours and not 28, and why every coral is pink or lilac: a yellow coral under water snaps to the land's lime green.

Not there yet: reflections, a sky to reflect, waves that move the surface, fish, foam around rocks that break the surface, and anything for the open sea. The tint uses straight-down depth, not the true distance through the water, so steep viewing angles are slightly too clear.

## Data (`data/`)

Exported from the browser build.

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `world_pos.bin` | Splat positions, three floats each |
| `world_col.bin` | Colour and glow flag, four bytes each |
| `world_misc.bin` | Sway, group, size, phase, four bytes each. For the lake's surface splats (group 5) the sway slot holds depth |
| `world_nrm.bin` | Facing direction and shape code, four signed bytes each |
| `world.json` | Map cells, palette, ground height grid, water level |
| `calf.bin`, `calf.json` | The calf's splats, bones and joint positions |
| `hero.bin`, `hero.json` | The player's splats, bones, joint positions and bone parents |
| `palette.json` | 32 colours: 24 fitted to the land and the characters, plus 8 chosen by hand for the lake |

## Known gaps

- Only the calf, and only part of its behaviour. No other creatures.
- The player does not blink (the eyes are part of the head's surface) and has no ankle or finger joints.
- Lighting is still baked into the splat colours.
- Tested only with a software renderer. Real frame rate is unknown until it runs on a GPU.
- Found on the way: byte-packed custom vertex attributes only delivered one channel in the Compatibility renderer, so the data is sent as floats.
